# Privacy for Small Business Checklist

*Print this page. Check off each item as you complete it. Designed to print on one or two sheets of paper.*

---

## 📋 Customer Data

- [ ] List every tool that holds customer data (store, email, invoices, spreadsheets, scheduling)
- [ ] Write down what data each tool stores and who can access it
- [ ] Stop collecting information you don't actually need
- [ ] Never store full credit card numbers — let your payment processor handle them
- [ ] Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) on email, store, accounting, and website admin accounts
- [ ] Use unique, strong passwords for every business account (use a password manager)
- [ ] Limit customer data access to people who need it for their jobs
- [ ] Back up customer data to an encrypted, access-controlled location
- [ ] Make a vendor list and note what customer data each vendor sees
- [ ] Set up a process for handling customer data deletion requests
- [ ] Know which records you must keep for tax or legal reasons

## 🤖 AI Tool Safety

- [ ] Never paste customer names, emails, orders, or support tickets into AI chat
- [ ] Never paste contracts, NDAs, or financials into AI chat
- [ ] Never paste passwords, API keys, or credentials into AI chat
- [ ] Anonymize before pasting (use "Customer A", "customer@example.com")
- [ ] Turn off "improve model" / training in AI tool settings where available
- [ ] Use local models (Ollama, LM Studio, GPT4All) for sensitive work
- [ ] Don't use AI meeting notetakers on customer calls without customer consent
- [ ] Write a team rule: what is and isn't okay to paste into AI

## 📄 Simple Policies

- [ ] Publish a short privacy policy on your website (what you collect, why, who sees it, how to contact you)
- [ ] Link the privacy policy from your site footer and checkout pages
- [ ] Set a data retention schedule (how long you keep each type of customer data)
- [ ] Pick a yearly date to review and delete outdated customer data
- [ ] Give employees/contractors a one-page customer-data handling guide
- [ ] Use the vendor assessment checklist before adding tools that touch customer data
- [ ] Write a short incident response plan (who to notify, what to do if data is exposed)

## 🔄 Quarterly Maintenance

- [ ] Review customer data access — remove access for people who no longer need it
- [ ] Delete outdated customer data per your retention schedule
- [ ] Review vendor list — remove tools you no longer use
- [ ] Confirm 2FA is still enabled on critical accounts
- [ ] Check for data breach news involving tools you use (haveibeenpwned.com)
- [ ] Update this checklist

## 🚨 Emergency: If Customer Data Is Exposed

1. **Secure accounts** — change passwords, revoke access immediately
2. **Find out what was exposed** — which data, how many customers
3. **Stop the leak** — remove exposed files, disable compromised accounts
4. **Notify affected customers** — explain what happened and what they should do
5. **Report if required** — regulators or affected individuals, depending on your location/industry
6. **Fix the cause** — update policies, add 2FA, train your team
